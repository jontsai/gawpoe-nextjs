<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Legal claims spotlight tech giant’s dominance in online advertising, both as a buyer and seller</h2>



<p>By&nbsp;<a href="https://www.wsj.com/news/author/patience-haggin">Patience Haggin</a>Follow</p>



<p>Updated&nbsp;May 17, 2019 3:55 pm&nbsp;ET</p>



<p><a href="https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/GOOG">Alphabet</a>&nbsp;Inc.’s&nbsp;<a href="https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/GOOG">GOOG&nbsp;-0.28%decrease; red down pointing triangle</a>&nbsp;Google has agreed to refund advertisers for ads purchased on its ad marketplaces that ran on websites with fraudulent traffic, following a lawsuit claiming the tech giant was withholding the payments, according to court documents and people familiar with the situation.</p>



<p>In 2017, Google&nbsp;<a href="https://www.wsj.com/articles/google-issuing-refunds-to-advertisers-over-fake-traffic-plans-new-safeguard-1503675395?mod=article_inline" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">offered limited refunds</a>&nbsp;to hundreds of marketers and ad agencies who had purchased ad space through its online ad-buying tool after the company discovered a wide-ranging fraud scheme, which resulted in ads running on sites whose traffic was artificially boosted by “bots”—software programs that mimic the website clicks of humans.</p>
