<p><a href="https://techcrunch.com/author/megan-rose-dickey/">Megan Rose Dickey</a> 10:41 AM PST • January 15, 2019</p>



<p id="speakable-summary">Getaround is getting around the courthouse. One of the car-sharing startup’s early investors,&nbsp;<a href="https://angel.co/geoffrey-shmigelsky">Geoffrey Shmigelsky</a>, is suing the company, alleging fraud and unfair conduct.</p>



<p>“Our client supported Getaround and Mr. Zaid from the very start, only to be swindled out of $1.785 million that went straight into the pockets of Mr. Zaid’s family and friends, as we allege,”&nbsp;Gaw | Poe LLP Partner Samuel Song said in a statement. “Our client deserved better than this from a person he had supported and trusted for years, and we’ll do what it takes to get what rightfully belongs to him.”</p>



<p>Getaround, however, says “these claims are totally unfounded and we’re looking to get the case dismissed,” Getaround Director of Marketing Communications Jacqueline Tanzella told TechCrunch over the phone.</p>



<p>Specifically, the lawsuit alleges Getaround executives tricked Shmigelsky into selling his shares to their friends and family for $1.79 million less “than what they knew they were worth.” Early last year, investors became interested in purchasing&nbsp;Shmigelsky’s shares, the lawsuit states. But because Getaround is still a private company with scarce public financial information, “they struggled to value Plaintiff’s shares.” That’s when&nbsp;Shmigelsky said he asked Getaround CEO Sam Zaid for the information.</p>
