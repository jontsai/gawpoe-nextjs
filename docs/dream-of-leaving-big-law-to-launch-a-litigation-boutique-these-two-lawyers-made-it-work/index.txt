<p>“Lawyers are risk-averse by nature, but I knew if I didn’t give this a try, I’d regret it for the rest of my life,” said Randolph Gaw, whose litigation boutique Gaw Poe celebrated its fourth birthday with a pair of wins.</p>



<p>By Jenna Greene | Updated on June 06, 2018</p>



<p>The original version of this story was published on&nbsp;<a href="https://www.law.com/litigationdaily/litigationdaily/">Litigationdaily/litigationdaily</a></p>



<p>Four years ago, Mark Poe left the security—and confines—of&nbsp;<a href="https://www.law.com/law-firm-profile?id=216&amp;name=Morrison-Foerster" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Morrison &amp; Foerster</a>, where he’d just been promoted to of counsel, to open a litigation boutique with his old Stanford Law classmate Randolph Gaw, a former associate at&nbsp;<a href="https://www.law.com/law-firm-profile?id=499&amp;name=O%27Melveny" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">O’Melveny &amp; Myers</a>.</p>



<p>Last week, their&nbsp;<a href="https://www.gawpoe.com/">San Francisco-based firm</a>&nbsp;got a birthday present of sorts: Poe on May 31 had two different eight-figure appeals affirmed by two different appellate courts. Sweeter still, these were literally his first and second cases after leaving MoFo to launch Gaw Poe.</p>
