<p>Gaw | Poe LLP files lawsuit against United Airlines on behalf of client alleging that its negligence led to injuries in connection with United Flight 931 on October 27, 2018.</p>



<p>NEWS PROVIDED BY <strong>Gaw | Poe LLP</strong></p>



<p>Jan 23, 2019, 12:40 ET</p>



<p>SAN FRANCISCO,&nbsp;Jan. 23, 2019&nbsp;/PRNewswire-PRWeb/ &#8212; The&nbsp;San Francisco&nbsp;law firm Gaw | Poe LLP today filed a lawsuit against United Airlines, Inc. on behalf of a passenger on its&nbsp;October 27, 2018, Flight 931 from&nbsp;Chicago&nbsp;to&nbsp;London. As alleged in the lawsuit, on that day, Flight 931 made an emergency descent from 40,000 feet to land at the Goose Bay airport in&nbsp;Canada. The lawsuit alleges that the cause of this emergency landing was because the cockpit window for the Boeing 767-300 had shattered, with two of its three layers having been completely compromised.</p>



<p>And as alleged in the lawsuit, the reason why Flight 931&#8217;s cockpit window shattered was because a United mechanic had over-torqued the bolts of the cockpit window. As alleged in the lawsuit, however, United&#8217;s customer service personnel have already been instructed to respond to inquiries by explaining that the accident was caused by a &#8220;bird strike.&#8221; In addition, as alleged in the lawsuit, United Airlines had also inexplicably prevented any passengers from deplaning Flight 931 for nearly 8 hours following its emergency landing.</p>



<p>The lawsuit brings a claim arising under the Montreal Convention on behalf of Gaw | Poe&#8217;s client for his bodily and mental injuries suffered as a result of this incident. The lawsuit seeks to hold United Airlines both strictly liable, and liable in negligence pursuant to the terms of that treaty. &#8220;My client is still struggling with the injuries he suffered, and he deserves fair compensation for them,&#8221; said&nbsp;Randolph Gaw, plaintiffs&#8217; attorney and a founder of Gaw | Poe LLP. Gaw added, &#8220;United made billions of dollars in profits last year, and it&#8217;s insulting that they think a&nbsp;$500&nbsp;voucher is a fair trade for what happened to my client.&#8221;</p>



<p>The case is captioned&nbsp;Theodore Liaw&nbsp;v. United Airlines, Inc., et al., Case No. 3:19-CV-00396, and is filed in&nbsp;the United States&nbsp;District Court for the Northern District of&nbsp;California.</p>
